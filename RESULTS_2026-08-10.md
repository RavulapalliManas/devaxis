## 1. What the experiment is

A 6-layer, 4.95 M-parameter GPT (closed vocabulary, fixed-length sequences) is trained on a
synthetic binding task. Each sequence declares **K** entity→obligation bindings in a RECAP
block, then queries one of them.

Two supervision channels are separated by the loss mask, and **only** by the loss mask:

- the **RECAP block is always supervised** — the "read" circuit,
- the **answer token is supervised with probability *p*** — the "use" circuit.

Because the data is a pure function of `(stream, step)`, two runs that differ only in *p*
see **identical bytes**. Every comparison in this document is therefore a comparison of
supervision schedules on the same data, never of different data.

**Emergence criterion, everywhere:** `crit = chance + 0.5 × (1 − chance)`, taken from each
run's own recorded `chance_query_ctx`. K=2 (chance 0.50) must clear 0.75; K=4 (chance 0.25)
must clear 0.625. Never hard-coded — see the bug in §9.6.

**χ (chi), the plasticity instrument:** Δ`gold_mass` between a pulse fork (*p*=1) and its
control fork (*p*=0) launched from the **same checkpoint on identical bytes**, at the matched
last logit eval. χ measures how much a burst of supervision can still move the answer
distribution. It is a *capacity* measure, not a performance measure.

## 4. The main result — same behaviour, different futures

**Two K=2 populations that are behaviourally indistinguishable have measurably different
futures.**

- **Never-formed:** forked *before* emergence, *p*=0 for 10,000 steps, then *p*=1.
- **Formed-then-collapsed:** forked *after* emergence, *p*=0 for 10,000 steps (the circuit
  collapses), then *p*=1.

**The premise was verified at full n before the contrast was run** (this is the load-bearing
check, and it passes):

| arm | n | query_ctx at rescue onset | above crit (0.75) |
|---|---|---|---|
| never-formed | 40 | 0.453–0.588, median **0.500** | **0/40** |
| formed-collapsed | 40 | 0.463–0.535, median **0.506** | **0/40** |

Chance is 0.500. Both arms sit on it. `recap` is **1.000** in both — the read circuit is
perfect in every run, so the two populations are indistinguishable on *every* behavioural
measure available, not merely on the headline one.

**They then diverge:**

| | recovered | KM median (rescued steps) |
|---|---|---|
| never-formed | **31/40** | 12,500 |
| formed-collapsed | **18/40** | **never reached** |

- registered paired test — exact McNemar, discordant 17 vs 4: **p = 0.0072**
  (`analysis/mcnemar_k2.py`)
- corrected primary — log-rank on the rescued clock: **χ² = 5.960, p = 0.0146**
  (`analysis/km_rescued.py`)

Survival analysis is the *corrected* primary, not a second opinion: the arms had unequal
follow-up (30k vs ~59k rescued steps) while observed recoveries ran to 36k. That censoring
bias was mine, it initially favoured the wrong arm, and §9.2 records how it was found and
fixed.

**Reading.** Having once had the capability and lost it is worse than never having had it.
The formed-then-collapsed state is the more absorbing one. Behaviour does not identify it.

## 5. The foreclosure clock — plasticity dies before behaviour changes

χ measured on deprived trajectories, pulse forks against same-bytes trajectory controls.
Pre-declared in `tools/make_k4expand.py` **before** the expansion data existed: primary
probe δ=8000, pooled n=16, two-sided exact sign test, ≥13/16 required.

`analysis/chi_paired.py`:

| probe | declines positive | exact sign p |
|---|---|---|
| **δ = 8000 (PRIMARY)** | **15/16** | **0.0005** |
| δ = 4000 (secondary) | 14/16 | 0.0042 |

Both clear the declared bar; no probe-switching after the data. An earlier n=8 look gave
7/8, p = 0.070 — that was a sample-size ceiling, not a weak effect.

**The decay profile** (`analysis/chi_matrix.py`, χ mean ± sd):

| deprivation age | δ=1000 | δ=4000 | δ=8000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** (positive control) | +0.0122 ± 0.0164 (8) | +0.1907 ± 0.1956 (16) | **+0.4835 ± 0.2819 (16)** |
| 8,000 | +0.0006 ± 0.0207 (8) | +0.0028 ± 0.0124 (16) | **−0.0011 ± 0.0145 (16)** |
| 16,000 | −0.0036 ± 0.0169 (8) | +0.0006 ± 0.0148 (16) | +0.0011 ± 0.0148 (16) |
| 20,000 | +0.0057 ± 0.0169 (8) | +0.0056 ± 0.0204 (8) | — |

Plasticity is **gone by deprivation age 8,000** and never returns. The positive control at
age 0 (+0.48) is far above the instrument floor, so the null at age 8,000 is a measured
zero, not a failure to measure.

**Plasticity is not a decaying resource.** Along *normal* (supervised) development it
**grows** (`analysis/chi_normal.py`):

| age | 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,000 | 4,000 | 8,000 | 12,000 | 16,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| χ | +0.0004 | +0.0000 | +0.0155 | +0.0122 | **+0.2347** | +0.1264 | **+0.3739** |
| n | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 3 | 3 |

Same clock, opposite sign, depending only on whether the answer channel is supervised.
Foreclosure is caused by *deprivation*, not by age. This is the control that makes the
clock result mean anything.

## 6. τ_c(K) — the foreclosure boundary moves with load

`analysis/results_tables.py` §A. Total gap of length τ, then full supply restored.

| K | τ=2,000 | τ=5,000 | τ=10,000 | τ=20,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 8/8 | 8/8 | **8/8** | — |
| 3 | 8/8 | 7/8 | **0/8** | — |
| 4 | 4/8 | 2/8 | — | **0/8** |

τ_c(3) ∈ (5,000, 10,000]. K=2 survives a 10,000-step gap intact. K=4 is already
half-lethal at τ=2,000.

For K=4 the picture is confirmed independently across gap *onsets*, all with a 10,000-step
gap (`early` and `window` phases): onsets 1,000 / 2,000 / 3,000 / 4,000 / 8,000 / 12,000 give
**2/46 recoveries in total**, both at the earliest onset. A 10,000-step interruption is
effectively unrecoverable for K=4 wherever it lands.

**The higher the load, the shorter the interruption a capability survives.**

## 7. The two supply axes — and the corrected trickle result

`p_after` named **different variables** in this codebase and in the h100-clocks codebase.
Untangling that (§9.3) leaves two independent axes:

- **p_during** — supply held *through* the interruption. Does the circuit **survive**?
- **p_rescue** — supply applied *after* a total gap. Once collapsed, can it be **revived**?

### 7.1 Retention: what it costs to keep a capability alive

`analysis/results_tables.py` §C — supply held through, no rescue.

| K | p=0.005 | p=0.01 | p=0.02 | p=0.03 | p=0.04 | p=0.05 | p=0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 1/8 | 4/8 | — | 7/8 | — | — | — |
| 4 | — | 1/7 | 4/7 | **7/7** | 7/7 | 7/7 | 7/7 |

A **3% duty cycle** holds the circuit indefinitely at both loads; 1% mostly does not. The
threshold is sharp and it scales with load.

### 7.2 Revival: what it costs to bring one back

`analysis/results_tables.py` §D, K=2, forked post-emergence, τ=10,000.

| p_rescue | 0.01 | 0.02 | 0.05 | 0.20 | 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| recovered | 0/8 | 0/8 | 0/8 | **4/8** | 18/40 |

Pooled ≤0.05 (**0/24**) vs 0.20 (**4/8**): **Fisher p = 0.0019**. There is a **revival rate
threshold** near 0.20 — an order of magnitude above the ~1–3% that suffices for retention.
**Prevention is roughly ten times cheaper than cure.**

The pre-registered "low restore rates work *better*" branch is **refuted**: 0.01, 0.02 and
0.05 are all 0/8. The registered 0.02-vs-1.00 comparison gives Fisher p = 1.00.
The 0.20-vs-1.00 apparent drop is post-hoc and **unsupported** (p = 1.00).

### 7.3 Severity interacts with rate

| contrast | result | Fisher p |
|---|---|---|
| τ=2,500 vs τ=10,000, both at p_rescue=0.20 | 8/8 vs 4/8 | 0.077 |
| τ=3,000 vs τ=10,000, both at p_rescue=1.0 | 7/8 vs 18/40 | 0.050 |

Severity bites, but neither contrast is decisive at this n, and the second is unpaired
across phases. Reported as suggestive.

### 7.4 The trickle — **corrected today, and narrower than I reported**

The question: does a trickle of supply held *through* the gap change the future of a model
that still ends the gap at chance?

Three doses were run on **the same 8 parents (s24–s31)**, each with full restore afterwards.
All three recovered 8/8. **But they are not the same experiment**, and yesterday I pooled
them. Checking the collapse state at rescue onset:

| p_during | recovered | **collapsed below crit at rescue onset** | mean query_ctx over last 5k of gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.005** | 8/8 | **8/8** | 0.494 – 0.612 |
| 0.01 | 8/8 | **2/8** | 0.505 – 0.892 |
| 0.02 | 8/8 | **0/8** | 0.733 – 0.961 |
| 0 (total gap) | 1/8 | 8/8 | 0.484 – 0.518 |

**At 0.01 and 0.02 the circuit largely never collapsed.** Those cells are the retention
result of §7.1 restated — not evidence about hidden state. Pooling all three into "24/24 vs
18/40, Fisher p = 2e-6" conflated maintenance with latent preservation. **That pooled
headline is withdrawn.**

**What survives is the 0.005 cell, and it is the more interesting claim anyway:**

> At p_during = 0.005, all 8 models sat **at chance** through the gap (0.494–0.612 against a
> chance level of 0.500) with `recap` = 1.000, behaviourally indistinguishable from the
> total-gap arm — and recovered **8/8**, where the *same 8 seeds* under a total gap recovered
> **1/8**.
> Paired exact McNemar (discordant 7 vs 0): **p = 0.0156**. Fisher: p = 0.0014.

A duty cycle of 0.005 over 10,000 steps is **≈50 supervised step-equivalents**. It buys no
visible behaviour whatsoever, and it changes the future.

Two honest caveats, both of which I checked rather than assumed:

1. The comparison is **paired on the same seeds**, which matters here: those 8 seeds are
   unusually hard — 1/8 under a total gap against 18/40 (45%) for the full 40-seed arm. The
   paired contrast is therefore **conservative**, not inflated. Why that tranche is harder is
   unexplained and worth knowing.
2. n = 8. The effect is large and paired, but it is one cell on one load at one gap length.

**And it does not generalise to formation.** The same trickle applied at K=4 *before*
emergence (`zero` phase, forked pre-emergence, `analysis/supply_map.py`) does **not** rescue:

| K=4, pre-emergence, p_during | 0.001 | 0.005 | 0.01 |
|---|---|---|---|
| emerged after restore | 2/8 | 1/8 | 0/8 |

No dose-response, all low. A trickle preserves a capability that **already exists**; it does
not help one **form** through an interruption. That is a real boundary on the claim, and it is
the sharpest reason not to state §7.4 as a general principle about supervision schedules.

## 8. Negative results

These are reported at the same weight as the positives. Several were pre-registered
predictions that failed.

**8.1 Repair is 0-for-4.** Nothing tried restores plasticity to a foreclosed network.

*Transplant* (`analysis/results_tables.py` §E) — plastic↔foreclosed layer-group chimeras, χ per arm:

| arm | χ (mean ± sd, n=8) | % of full swap |
|---|---|---|
| noop (fresh-optimizer control) | −0.0046 ± 0.0124 | −1.5% |
| lower (emb, b0–b2) | +0.0662 ± 0.0997 | 22.0% |
| upper (b3–b5, out) | +0.0643 ± 0.1073 | 21.4% |
| **full** | **+0.3009 ± 0.1664** | 100% |
| revup (foreclosed upper → plastic base) | +0.1846 ± 0.2789 | 61.4% |

Halves recover ~22% each and are **sub-additive** (0.1305 = 43% of full). The confound guard
passed: **0/8 runs in every arm** had pre-pulse behaviour above criterion, so no arm restored
current performance instead of plasticity. `revup` retains 61% of plastic χ — foreclosure
does **not** transfer as a module. **Foreclosure is distributed, not localised.**

*Symmetry breaking* (`analysis/results_tables.py` §F) — antisymmetric perturbation of
near-duplicate MLP units, both at the early χ-dead state (12,000) and the late duplicated
state (24,000). Every arm is a null:

| state | noop | symb | randp | symfar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12,000 | +0.0106 ± 0.0146 | −0.0030 ± 0.0231 | −0.0021 ± 0.0143 | +0.0111 ± 0.0179 |
| 24,000 | −0.0046 ± 0.0124 | +0.0030 ± 0.0184 | +0.0005 ± 0.0241 | −0.0042 ± 0.0109 |

(The 24,000 `noop` cell reproduces the transplant `noop` cell to four decimals — the same
computation reached independently through two separately built phases. An unplanned
replication of the determinism guarantee.)

*Optimizer and schedule interventions* (`mech` phase): plain / optimizer-reset / LR-rewarm /
both, after a 12,000-step gap — **0/7 in all four arms**. Foreclosure is not an artefact of
optimizer state or learning-rate schedule.

*Weight decay* (`wd0` phase): with weight decay disabled, baseline still emerges 7/8, and
rescue after 10k and 20k gaps is still **0/8 and 0/8**. Not a weight-decay artefact.

**8.2 The cloned-unit manifold is refuted as a cause — by its own timing.**
Duplicate units are real and deprivation-specific: at step 24,000, duplicate fraction is
**0.4074 in deprived vs 0.0079 in age-matched supervised** checkpoints. But the trajectory
(`analysis/unit_collapse.py`) shows duplication is **flat at 0.0000 until deprivation age
15,000**, first moving at 16,000 (0.0031) and reaching 0.4074 at 20,000 — whereas **χ is
already dead at 8,000**. The proposed cause arrives ~8,000 steps after the effect.
Effective rank does fall monotonically (150.6 → 23.2) and remains a candidate; duplication is
a downstream signature.

**8.3 Matched-dose continuity: refuted.** Holding dose fixed at 300 p-step-equivalents in
[4k, 14k) and varying only its temporal shape:

| arm | max gap | emerged |
|---|---|---|
| cont003 | 0 | 1/8 |
| micro | 970 | 0/8 |
| bstart | 9,700 | 0/8 |
| bend | 9,700 | 0/8 |

The registered sharpest signature (micro ≫ bstart at identical dose) gives **Fisher p =
1.00**. At this dose, *when* supervision arrives does not matter, because no shape is enough.
The continuity pillar is not supported by this experiment.

**8.4 Re-forming is slower than forming — the collapsed state is damage, not dormancy.**
`analysis/savings_k2.py`: of 40 formed-collapsed runs, 18 re-formed; savings were **positive
in only 3/18**, median **−0.44** (re-forming took ~1.4× the de-novo time). 22 never re-formed
within 30,000–60,000 rescued steps. The contrasting induction result from the h100 lab
(+92.9% savings, 12/12 faster) has the **opposite sign**, supporting genuine damage in
binding-use collapse rather than retained-but-silent structure.

**8.5 The DD recurrence factorial — null.** This was the *originally planned* science
campaign: recurrence {exact, paraphrase} × spacing {short, long} × order {fixed, scrambled},
n=5 per cell against an n=8 baseline (KM median 9,750). Per-cell log-rank against baseline:
raw p from 0.197 to 0.943; **every Holm-corrected p = 1.00** except one at 0.943.
No cell differs from baseline. Reported because it was pre-planned and run, not because it
worked.

## 9. Corrections on the record

Every one of these is a case where an earlier statement of mine was wrong. They are listed
so the same error is not re-derived from an old summary.

1. **"17× speedup from `sync_every`" — retracted.** The A/A test measured **0.99×**.
2. **The n=24 flagship near-claim — self-corrected.** The formed arm had 30k follow-up vs
   never-formed's ~59k while recoveries ran to 36k: a censoring bias I introduced. The
   corrected primary (KM/log-rank) read p = 0.216. Fixed by horizon-matching, then confirmed
   at n=40 (§4).
3. **"Binding = damage vs induction = retained structure" — withdrawn as stated.** It
   compared two experiments that measured different variables (`p_after` names different
   things in the two codebases). The two-axis map in §7 replaced it.
4. **"Mastery protects plasticity" — retracted.** It came from a gap-unmatched comparison.
5. **Cloned manifold as the *cause* of foreclosure — refuted** by its own timing (§8.2).
6. **`supply_map.py` hard-coded `held() >= 0.75` — fixed today.** That is the K=2 bar; applied
   to K=4 (crit 0.625) it was stricter than the criterion used everywhere else and
   under-reported two maintenance runs (p=0.01 read 0/7 instead of 1/7; p=0.02 read 3/7
   instead of 4/7). §7.1 uses the corrected values.
7. **The pooled trickle headline (24/24 vs 18/40, p = 2e-6) — withdrawn today** (§7.4). It
   pooled two doses that never collapsed with one that did. The surviving claim is the
   0.005 cell, paired, p = 0.0156.
8. **k4xtraj / k4xpulse were reported pending when they had completed** — a glob bug in an
   ad-hoc status script, caught by the full phase audit.

## 11. What this does and does not establish

**Established on this task, at this scale:**

- A capability can be lost in a way that **behaviour cannot detect** but that changes what the
  network can later learn (§4, §7.4).
- The capacity to re-learn dies on a **measurable clock**, ~8,000 steps of deprivation, and
  dies **before** any behavioural or structural signature appears (§5).
- The clock is **load-dependent**: τ_c falls as K rises (§6).
- **Retention is ~10× cheaper than revival** (~1–3% duty vs a ~20% threshold), and a duty
  cycle too small to produce any visible behaviour (0.005) still preserves the future (§7).
- Along normal development, plasticity **grows**; only deprivation destroys it (§5).

**Not established, and not to be claimed:**

- **Mechanism.** Repair is 0-for-4 and the leading structural hypothesis was refuted by its own
  timing. Effective-rank collapse remains a candidate; it has not been shown causal.
- **Generality.** One synthetic task, one architecture, 4.95 M parameters. Nothing here
  measures task families, model scale, or naturalistic capability packets.
- **Any claim about real pretraining runs.** The step counts, duty cycles and thresholds are
  properties of this task at this scale, not transferable constants.

**The three things I would run next, ranked by information per unit time:**

1. **Extend the 0.005 cell** — the one clean latent-preservation result rests on n=8 at one
   load and one gap length. Run it at K=4 and at τ=2,500, and find the dose floor where
   preservation fails. This is the result most worth being certain about, and the box is idle.
2. **Bisect the tranche heterogeneity** — seeds s24–s31 recover 1/8 under a total gap against
   45% overall. Something distinguishes them, and it is currently unexplained.
3. **Effective rank as a causal handle** — it is the last structural candidate standing (§8.2).
   A rank-restoring intervention that fails would close the mechanism question here honestly;
   one that works would open it.
